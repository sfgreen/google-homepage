Background: Dynamic left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO) is a reversible, preload-dependent cause of shock easily missed when ejection fraction is preserved and worsened by vasopressors.
Case: A 69-year-old woman with obesity and recent GLP-1 agonist weight loss presented with syncope and shock after heat exposure. Prior echocardiogram was normal. Troponin rose to 787 ng/L with dynamic ECG changes. Shock worsened despite fluids, norepinephrine, and vasopressin. Echocardiography showed basal hyperkinesis, apical akinesis, and dynamic LVOTO (45 mmHg at rest, 110 mmHg with Valsalva). Norepinephrine was stopped; phenylephrine, fluids, and then metoprolol restored hemodynamics. Angiography showed no coronary disease. Cardiac MRI showed basal anteroseptal thickening (15.6 mm) with LVOT flow acceleration, no subaortic membrane, mid-apical edema without scar, and EF 54% (Figure). Follow-up echocardiogram on beta-blockade showed no resting or provoked gradient.
Discussion: Dehydration shrank the LV cavity while catecholamine stress and Takotsubo-related basal hyperkinesis increased contractility, unmasking latent obstruction from basal septal hypertrophy. Volume, pure vasoconstriction, and beta-blockade, avoiding inotropes and diuretics, reversed the physiology.
Conclusion: In shock with a hyperdynamic heart, preserved EF does not exclude a mechanical cause. Early LVOT assessment and gradient-guided therapy can break the cycle of collapse.
