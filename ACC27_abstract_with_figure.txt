Background: Dynamic left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO) is a reversible, preload-dependent cause of shock that is easily missed when ejection fraction is preserved. GLP-1 agonists, which blunt thirst and cause gastrointestinal losses, may be an emerging precipitant.
Case: A 69-year-old woman with obesity had lost XX lbs over X months on a GLP-1 agonist, with poor oral intake. After heat exposure she presented with syncope and shock. Prior echocardiogram was normal. Shock worsened despite fluids, norepinephrine, and vasopressin. Echocardiography showed basal hyperkinesis, apical akinesis, and dynamic LVOTO (45 mmHg at rest, 110 mmHg with Valsalva). Norepinephrine was stopped; phenylephrine, fluids, then metoprolol restored hemodynamics. Angiography showed no coronary disease. Cardiac MRI showed basal anteroseptal thickening (15.6 mm) with LVOT flow acceleration, no subaortic membrane, mid-apical edema without scar, and EF 54% (Figure). Follow-up echocardiogram on beta-blockade showed no gradient.
Discussion: Drug-induced volume depletion shrank the LV cavity; shock triggered a catecholamine surge and Takotsubo basal hyperkinesis; together they unmasked latent obstruction from a thick basal septum. Norepinephrine fed the loop; volume, pure vasoconstriction, and beta-blockade broke it.
Conclusion: Rapid GLP-1 weight loss can convert a benign septal bulge into a shock substrate. In vasopressor-refractory shock with preserved EF, assess the LVOT early.
