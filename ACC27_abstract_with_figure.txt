Background: Dynamic left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO) is a reversible, preload-dependent cause of shock easily missed when ejection fraction is preserved. GLP-1 agonists, which blunt thirst and raise heart rate, are a new substrate.
Case: A 69-year-old woman with obesity, losing weight rapidly on a GLP-1 agonist, presented with syncope and shock after heat exposure. Prior echocardiogram was normal. Shock worsened despite fluids, norepinephrine, and vasopressin. Echocardiography showed basal hyperkinesis, apical akinesis, and dynamic LVOTO (45 mmHg at rest, 110 mmHg with Valsalva). Norepinephrine was stopped; phenylephrine, fluids, and then metoprolol restored hemodynamics. Angiography showed no coronary disease. Cardiac MRI showed basal anteroseptal thickening (15.6 mm) with LVOT flow acceleration, no subaortic membrane, mid-apical edema without scar, and EF 54% (Figure). Follow-up echocardiogram on beta-blockade showed no resting or provoked gradient.
Discussion: Pharmacologic hypovolemia from GLP-1-mediated anorexia and blunted thirst, plus heat shrank the LV cavity, while catecholamine stress and Takotsubo-related basal hyperkinesis unmasked latent obstruction from basal septal hypertrophy. Volume, pure vasoconstriction, and beta-blockade, avoiding inotropes and diuretics, reversed it.
Conclusion: As GLP-1 therapy expands, dehydrated patients with preserved EF and a thick basal septum may present with vasopressor-refractory shock. Assess the LVOT early.
