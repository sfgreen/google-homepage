Background: Dynamic left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO) is a reversible, preload-dependent cause of shock that is easily missed when ejection fraction is preserved and prior imaging is normal. Standard vasopressor escalation can worsen it.
Case: A 69-year-old woman with hypertension, hypothyroidism, and class II obesity with recent GLP-1 agonist weight loss presented with syncope and shock after heat exposure. Prior echocardiogram showed EF 60-65% without structural disease. Troponin rose to 787 ng/L with dynamic anterolateral ST depression and T-wave inversion. Shock worsened despite fluids and escalating norepinephrine and vasopressin. Echocardiography showed basal hyperkinesis with apical akinesis and dynamic LVOTO (gradient 45 mmHg at rest, 110 mmHg with Valsalva) and a basal septal ridge concerning for a subaortic membrane. Norepinephrine was stopped and phenylephrine with maintenance fluids started, improving hemodynamics. Metoprolol was added once MAP exceeded 65 mmHg. Angiography showed no coronary disease. Cardiac MRI showed asymmetric basal anteroseptal thickening (15.6 mm) with LVOT flow acceleration, no subaortic membrane, mid-apical T2 prolongation without late gadolinium enhancement, and EF 54%: a hypertrophic phenotype with resolving stress cardiomyopathy. Vasopressors were weaned off; follow-up echocardiogram on beta-blockade showed no resting or provoked gradient.
Discussion: Dehydration shrank the LV cavity while catecholamine stress and Takotsubo-related basal hyperkinesis increased contractility, unmasking latent obstruction from basal septal hypertrophy. Norepinephrine amplified the obstruction and deepened the shock. Volume, pure vasoconstriction, and beta-blockade, with inotropes and diuretics avoided, reversed the physiology. HCM evaluation and heart failure follow-up are warranted.
Conclusion: In shock with a hyperdynamic heart, preserved EF does not exclude a mechanical cause. Early LVOT assessment and gradient-guided therapy can break the cycle of collapse and liberate patients from vasopressors.
