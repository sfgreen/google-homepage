Background: Dynamic left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO) is a reversible, preload-dependent cause of shock that is easily missed when ejection fraction is preserved. GLP-1 receptor agonists blunt thirst and appetite, cause gastrointestinal losses, and raise resting heart rate: a new substrate for it.
Case: A 69-year-old woman with hypertension, hypothyroidism, and class II obesity presented with syncope and shock after heat exposure. She was losing weight rapidly on a GLP-1 receptor agonist with poor intake. Prior echocardiogram showed EF 60-65% without structural disease. Troponin rose to 787 ng/L with dynamic anterolateral ST depression and T-wave inversion. Shock worsened despite fluids and escalating norepinephrine and vasopressin. Echocardiography showed basal hyperkinesis with apical akinesis and dynamic LVOTO (gradient 45 mmHg at rest, 110 mmHg with Valsalva). Norepinephrine was stopped and phenylephrine with maintenance fluids started, improving hemodynamics. Metoprolol was added once MAP exceeded 65 mmHg. Angiography showed no coronary disease. Cardiac MRI showed asymmetric basal anteroseptal thickening (15.6 mm) with LVOT flow acceleration, no subaortic membrane, mid-apical T2 prolongation without late gadolinium enhancement, and EF 54%: a hypertrophic phenotype with resolving stress cardiomyopathy. Follow-up echocardiogram on beta-blockade showed no resting or provoked gradient.
Discussion: Three forces converged on a small, stiff ventricle. Pharmacologic hypovolemia from GLP-1-mediated anorexia, blunted thirst, and weight loss was compounded by heat exposure, shrinking the LV cavity. Catecholamine stress and Takotsubo-related basal hyperkinesis increased contractility, driving latent obstruction from basal septal hypertrophy. Volume, pure vasoconstriction, and beta-blockade, with inotropes and diuretics avoided, reversed the physiology.
Conclusion: As GLP-1 therapy expands, dehydrated patients with preserved EF and a thick basal septum may present with vasopressor-refractory shock. Early LVOT assessment and gradient-guided therapy can break the cycle of collapse.
